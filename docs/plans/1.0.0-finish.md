## 4. Technical plan

### 4.1 One clock, and everything that measures time (#107, #106, #123, #105, #122, #125)

**The clock (#107).** One value type in `NopeCore`, passed to `CameraModel` and from it to every model that reads time:

```swift
public struct NopeClock: Sendable {
    /// The wall clock: only for dates people see or that go into files.
    public var date: @Sendable () -> Date
    /// The monotonic clock: for everything that measures elapsed time.
    public var now: @Sendable () -> ContinuousClock.Instant
    /// Waits until a moment on the monotonic clock; throws when cancelled.
    public var sleep: @Sendable (ContinuousClock.Instant) async throws -> Void
    public static let system: NopeClock
}
```

- It replaces `CameraModel.wallClock`, the `currentDate`, `now` and `sleep` parameters of `CameraModel.init` and `ManualControlsModel.init`, `DriveModel`'s `sleep` and `now`, `Countdown`'s `sleep`, `PhotoSaver`'s `now` and `LiveActivityCoordinator`'s `now`. `SeriesScreenKeeper` already takes a monotonic `now` and a `sleep(until:)`; it takes the clock instead.
- A wait for a number of seconds becomes `sleep(now() + .seconds(n))`.
- Tests get one `FakeClock` (in `Tests/NopeCoreTests`) with `advance(by:)`, a separate `setDate(_:)`, and sleeps that wake when time is advanced, with an option to wake late. Tests replace this one thing.
- `ContinuousClock` keeps counting while the device sleeps, which is what keeps the decision of 2026-11-18: a timed series' end keeps running while the series is paused and while the phone sleeps.
- A check, so the two clocks stay apart: `scripts/check-architecture.sh` (or a unit test over the sources) fails on `Date()` or `Date.now` in `Sources/NopeCore` outside `NopeClock.swift`.
- This changes the public `NopeCore` API: `docs/api`, `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` and the Building an Interface example are updated in the same change.

**Timed series end (#106).** `IntervalProgress.timeSpan: ClosedRange<Date>?` becomes a monotonic deadline and a length:

- `IntervalProgress.deadline: ContinuousClock.Instant?` and `duration: Duration?`, set when the series starts.
- `secondsLeft(at:)` and `fractionDone(at:)` take a `ContinuousClock.Instant`. `hasTimeForAnotherPhoto`, `isIntervalTimeUp` and `wakeAtIntervalEnd` read the deadline.
- `endDate(clock:)` gives a `Date` for display and for the Live Activity as `clock.date() + secondsLeft`, worked out when it is needed, never as start plus duration.
- The interval card's `TimelineView` keeps ticking on dates but reads the time left from the clock at each tick.
- The Live Activity holds an absolute end date, as iOS requires. After a change of the device's clock that date is stale until the next update. The app observes the system's clock-change notification and asks the coordinator to send the content again; `NopeCore` gets a `clockChanged()` entry that the app calls.

**Start to start (#123).** In `ManualControlsModel.startInterval`:

- The loop keeps `nextStart: ContinuousClock.Instant`. After a photo that started at `s`, `nextStart = s + interval`.
- When the photo is done: if `now() >= nextStart`, the next photo starts at once and the series is marked behind schedule; otherwise it sleeps until `nextStart`. No photo is taken twice to catch up; the schedule continues from each photo's own start.
- The start time must be the moment the photo really began. `capture` waits inside `CameraModel` while the series is paused, so it returns when it started: `capture` changes from `() async -> Bool` to return an outcome with the start instant.
- A timed series takes another photo while its next scheduled start is before the deadline. That is today's rule in other words, so "At most N" stays true.
- `IntervalProgress` publishes `nextPhotoStart: ContinuousClock.Instant?` and `isBehindSchedule: Bool`. The card's `NextPhotoTiming`, which today counts from a `Date` taken in the view (`DriveControls.swift:774`), reads them instead. That is the last part of #106.

**Failed last photo (#105).** `capture`'s outcome is one of taken, time up while waiting, or failed. Only "time up" ends a series as finished; "failed" ends it as stopped, also when the deadline has passed.

**Self-timer (#122).** In `Countdown`:

- `start` sets `deadline = now() + seconds`. The loop shows `ceil(deadline − now())`, sleeps until the next whole second before the deadline, and fires when `now() >= deadline`.
- A late wake skips numbers; it does not move the deadline.
- `cancel()` and `run(seconds:)` keep their contracts.

**Live Activity (#125).** `lastSent` becomes a `ContinuousClock.Instant?`; the 30-second limit compares instants; `Date` stays only for the dismissal time of an ended activity.

### 4.2 The shutter path and bursts (#100, #101, #121)

**Why "one stream" is not enough by itself (#100).** `handleShutterPhase(.pressed)` awaits the whole burst (`CameraModel+Drive.swift:181`). If press and release went through one serial queue that awaited each event, the release would wait behind the burst it is meant to end. So the state change and the work are split:

- **A synchronous entry on `CameraModel`,** `shutter(_ phase: ShutterPhase, from source: ShutterSource)`, called straight from the view's closure and from the hardware event handler, with no `Task` in between. Both run on the main actor already, so calls arrive in the order the system made them.
- It changes the hold state at once, then starts the work in a task: a press starts the burst or the photo; a release or a cancel asks the burst to stop.
- **Each press gets a number.** A release carries the number of its press; a release whose press is not the current one is dropped. This covers any path that still delivers late.
- **Held is per source.** The on-screen shutter and the hardware buttons each have their own held flag; the burst is held while either is. Today one Boolean serves both, so a second button's release ends the first one's burst.
- The view's `Task { await model.send(.drive(.shutter(...))) }` and the same in `NopeApp.swift:209` and `NopeCaptureExtension.swift:79` go.

**Shutter goes away (#101).** `ShutterButton` already knows whether it reported a press (`pressWasReported`). It reports `.cancelled`:

- from `onDisappear`, when a press was reported;
- when `onPressChange` becomes `nil` while pressed (the camera left burst-shutter mode with the finger down; today the release is then never sent: `ShutterButton.swift:108`);
- and `CameraModel` cancels every hold when the app leaves the active state.

To verify before building: that a fold or unfold really removes the view while pressed, on a Duo simulator. If the system ends the touch first, `onChange(of: isPressed)` already fires and the new path is a second guard.

**Burst Limit (#121).**

- `NopeSettings.burstLimit: BurstLimit` (`off`, 3, 5, 10, 20, 50), stored by name, in `NopeSettingsSnapshot` and presets; an unknown stored value reads as Off.
- With a limit, `isBurstShutter` stays true but a press starts `takeBurst(frames: limit, start: .limited)`. A new `BurstStart.limited` does not stop on release; `isBurstStopRequested` ignores the held flag for it. A second press calls `requestBurstStop()`.
- `BurstEnd.countReached` exists (used by Siri's count); the design's "N of M photos saved" needs the asked-for count in `BurstSummary`.
- `BurstProgress` gains `limit: Int?` for the card.
- Siri and Shortcuts keep `start: .counted` and their own count.

### 4.3 Preview cost (#104)

This one starts with a measurement, because the fix depends on numbers nobody has.

1. **Measure, on a device, RAW only:** the burst rate with previews as today and with previews off (a Debug switch on `PhotoSaver`'s `makesPreview`), on each rear lens, 20 frames, three runs. And the time of one `preview(of:)` call for a DNG. Record both in `docs/TESTING.md`, "Burst rate".
2. **If previews cost less than about 10% of the rate,** change nothing but two cheap things: no preview while the app is not active, and none for a frame beyond the newest 20 of a counted burst, whose preview would be dropped at once (`SessionPhotos.maximumPreviews`).
3. **If they cost more,** the options, in order of preference:
   - a smaller preview for burst frames (the page's fitted size instead of 3072 px), decoded larger only when that photo is zoomed, which #103 builds anyway;
   - the preview of a burst's frames made after the burst from data still in memory, at most two frames at a time, as the burst's own memory rule allows;
   - no preview for burst frames but the last, the others showing their thumbnail in the viewer with the "only the thumbnail is left" state that exists.
   The third changes what the viewer shows and is the owner's call (decision 12, only if step 1 leads there).

A preview cannot be made later from the saved file: the app has add-only access to Photos and keeps no copy (AGENTS.md, "Photos never linger").

### 4.4 Siri: "tell nope to take a photo" (#126)

What is known: Release build of `int-1.0.0`, iPhone 16 Pro Max, iOS 27, app open on the camera. Siri answered "Sorry, something's wrong. Please try again." and no photo was saved. What is not known: the Assistant Access and Ask First settings at the time, and whether `perform()` ran at all. The first step is to find that out; the hypotheses are ranked by what the code makes likely.

**Hypotheses**

1. **The words did not reach the app's action.** The registered phrases are "Take a photo with nope.", "Take a picture with nope." and "Take a photo in … with nope." (`NopeShortcuts.swift:15–17`). "Tell nope to take a photo" is not one of them, and the app's name on the device is "nope." with a period and no alternative names or pronunciation hint (no `INAlternativeAppNames` in the project). If Siri routed the request somewhere else, `perform()` never ran and the generic sentence is Siri's own. Supported by: every refusal path in `perform()` returns a sentence, so a run that reached the gate with access off would have said "Assistant Access is off…".
2. **`perform()` threw or was ended around the move to the foreground.** `AssistantGate` calls `continueInForeground` without first checking `systemContext.currentMode.canContinueInForeground`, and swallows its error with `try?` (`AssistantGate.swift:63`). What that call does when the app is already in front, and from a voice request, has not been checked on a device (Assistant Access check 1, not run).
3. **The confirmation's control flow is swallowed.** `requestConfirmation` is wrapped in `do/catch` and every error becomes "cancelled" (`AssistantGate.swift:105–119`). Apple's guidance is to let its errors pass: some are how the system restarts or ends the run. With Ask First on Every Action, a voice request goes through this path.
4. **The time limit.** A background intent has about 30 seconds. The intent waits for the photo to be taken and saved; a custom delay of up to 60 seconds (`TakePhotoIntent.swift:44`) cannot fit at all. Unlikely for the reported case (no delay), but a real fault for long delays, and low light lengthens the exposure and the save.
5. **The answer could not be shown.** The dialog's sentences come from the `Honesty` table; if a sentence does not resolve in the app's bundle from the intent's process, the result could fail. Low.
6. **A crash in `perform()`** (a continuation resumed twice in `ShortcutInbox`, for example). The app was open and did not visibly quit, so low.

**Find out which, in this order**

| Step | Where | What it tells |
| --- | --- | --- |
| a. Say the registered phrase exactly, "Take a photo with nope", with Assistant Access on and Ask First set to Never | Device | Works: hypothesis 1. Fails the same way: 2, 4, 5 or 6 |
| b. Run Take Photo from the Shortcuts app, same settings | Device, and the simulator's Shortcuts app | Takes speech out. Works where (a) fails: Siri's routing or the name |
| c. Repeat (b) with Ask First on Every Action | Device, simulator | Fails only here: hypothesis 3 |
| d. Repeat (a) with Assistant Access off | Device | Should say "Assistant Access is off…". The generic sentence instead: `perform()` is not reached, or 5 |
| e. Watch the device log during (a): Console on a Mac, filtered to the app's process and to the App Intents subsystem; look for the intent starting, an error or a timeout | Device | Whether `perform()` ran, and how it ended |
| f. Add one `Logger` line at the start and the end of `perform()` and of `AssistantGate.answer` (private values, as the security check requires) in a Debug build, and repeat | Device | Where it stops |
| g. A delay of 10 s, then a custom delay of 40 s | Device | Hypothesis 4 |

On the simulator: Siri by voice is not available, so the simulator covers steps b and c only. The UI tests' Debug shortcut (`SimulatedShortcut.swift`) calls the gate without the system, which is why they pass while the device fails; it stays as it is.

**Likely fixes, by hypothesis** (each small; none is a new feature)

- 1: add the phrases people say, each with the app's name token: "Tell \(.applicationName) to take a photo" and "Take a photo using \(.applicationName)"; add `INAlternativeAppNames` with "nope" (no period) and a pronunciation hint. Adding phrases is safe; none is removed or renamed.
- 2: check `canContinueInForeground`; when it is false, throw `needsToContinueInForegroundError` with a sentence instead of going on; skip the call when the app is already active; do not swallow the error.
- 3: let `requestConfirmation`'s error pass out of `perform()`. The design's "Cancelled. Nothing changed." answer (device check 4) is then dropped, as that check's fallback column already allows.
- 4: answer when the timer starts ("Timer started: the photo will be taken in 40 seconds.") instead of waiting for the photo, for any delay over a safe limit; or limit Custom Delay to what fits. Decision 13.
- All: every path out of `perform()` that is a failure throws an error with its own sentence (a type that carries a localized message), so Siri never falls back to the generic one.

**Tests.** A regression test with Apple's App Intents testing framework for iOS 27, which runs an intent against the installed app out of process: Take Photo with access off, on with Never, and on with Every Action. It runs on the iOS 27 simulator leg only; the framework's API must be confirmed in the SDK before it is relied on. Unit tests for each new error sentence.

**Device check to add to `docs/TESTING.md`:** "Take a photo with nope." and the new phrases, with the app open, closed, and the phone locked; with access off, Never and Every Action; with a 10-second delay. Record Siri's exact words each time. This also runs Assistant Access checks 1, 2, 4, 5 and 11.

## 6. Decisions for the owner

Each has a recommendation. Until a decision is made, only the group named waits.

1. **#116: accept that mid-size iPhones gain only 2 to 5 points?** The picture cannot shrink and those screens have 3 to 11 spare points. The other way to make room is to lay the lens row over the lower edge of the picture, as the smallest iPhones already do. **Recommend: accept; do not cover the picture.** (Group D)
2. **#116: also give the shutter more room in the iPad and Duo column** (16 pt instead of 8 above and below it, when the column is at least 600 pt tall)? The issue names the iPhone only. **Recommend: yes;** it costs the picture nothing. (Group D)
3. **#117: how long does an untouched invitation card stay?** **Recommend: until Open or Dismiss is pressed,** with no count of launches and no timer. (Group H)
4. **#117: does Factory Defaults bring the card back?** **Recommend: no.** It is not a setting, and the guide is always in Settings. (Group H)
5. **#117: seven pages, or five** (without "Q and Settings" and "Apple Watch")? **Recommend: seven;** each is one or two short sentences, and the watch page was asked for. (Group H)
6. **#117: the row's name on the About screen:** "How to Use nope.", the guide's own title, or "How to Use Photivra Nope Camera", by the rule that About uses the full name? **Recommend: "How to Use nope."** in both places, as a title quoted from the interface. (Group H)
7. **#119: what "the thermal state is respected" means.** **Recommend: at the serious heat level the screen dims after 30 seconds whatever Dim After says,** as in Low Power Mode. Today heat changes nothing until the camera pauses at critical. (Group E)
8. **#119: does Never also stop an interval series or an Astro run from dimming?** **Recommend: no.** Series dim at once to save battery over hours; Dim After belongs to Keep Screen On. (Group E)
9. **#121: the Apple Watch shutter with a Burst Limit set.** **Recommend: unchanged from today,** and record what today is. (Group B)
10. **#123: say in Interval Shooting, before the series starts, when a manual shutter time is longer than the interval?** **Recommend: yes;** the app knows it then, and saying it late is less honest than saying it early. One string. (Group A)
11. **#74: close at release although its usability test and SUS scores were never done?** **Recommend: close #74, and file the usability test as its own issue for after 1.0.0.** Its checklists are answered by the design set and the accessibility audits.
12. **#104: only if the measurement shows previews cost more than about 10% of the burst rate and smaller previews are not enough: may burst frames other than the last show only a thumbnail in the viewer?** **Recommend: decide after the numbers;** smaller previews first. (Group B)
13. **#126: a Siri or Shortcuts delay longer than the system's time limit.** Answer when the timer starts rather than when the photo is saved, or cap Custom Delay (today up to 60 seconds) at what fits? **Recommend: answer when the timer starts,** for delays over 10 seconds; the photo's result then shows in the app as for a self-timer.
