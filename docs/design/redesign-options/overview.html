Redesign options

Three directions for the camera screen of nope., for the redesign in issue 74. Each is drawn as the same three screens on an iPhone in portrait (402 × 874 pt), so they compare directly: the camera screen in its default state, the Q quick menu open, and Astro ready to start. These are options for a decision, not the current design; the current design is the rest of this folder.

Scope: release 1.0.0 changes the interface of features the app already has. Nothing here needs a new capability. The Q menu holds the sixteen items of today's quick-controls tray, with Format added and Close-Up and Far Focus joined as Focus Range. Metering stays in Pro Tools because it applies only with Manual Controls on. Image stabilization is not offered because the app keeps it off. The golden-ratio grids are left out (issue 76), and the default format is RAW only (issue 80).

What all three share

  • The photo comes first. The 4:3 picture uses the full width of the screen in every option and is the same size in all three. They differ in what may rest on it and for how long.
  • One meaning per color. Teal only for a verified fact (the single-frame RAW seal), amber only for warnings, filled white for selected. Every state also has a symbol or words. Night mode is pure red on black, with on filled and off outlined.
  • One place per option. Each setting has one home. The Q menu and the pills are shortcuts to it and never behave differently from it.
  • Settings holds set-once choices (details saved in each photo, signing, file options, defaults). Anything used while shooting is on the camera screen or in the Q menu.
  • A Manual Controls button on the camera screen (issue 77), shown as words or letters and filled when on.
  • A processed-lens marker (issue 80) takes the place of the RAW seal on a lens without RAW: an amber triangle with words, in the same position in every option.

Each direction

A · Bands

Bands changes the least. The picture stays uncovered between two black bands, as it is today, and the bands hold fewer, clearer buttons. The sideways-scrolling tray becomes one Q panel in which every tile is visible at once with its name and value. Manual Controls gets its own button beside the lenses (issue 77). Use-case modes such as Astro are tiles in the panel, and once a mode is on, its values take a row in the lower band rather than a place on the picture. This direction keeps the habits of people who already use the app and carries the smallest build and test risk; the cost is that every mode is still two taps away and Settings stays at the top of the screen.

Accessibility, Siri and Shortcuts

  • VoiceOver order follows the screen from top to bottom: format, Settings, picture, Manual Controls, lenses, Quick Menu, Last Photo, Take Photo, camera flip.
  • The Q button is named "Quick Menu" for VoiceOver and Voice Control, with "Q" as a second spoken name, and it has a Large Content Viewer label. Each tile reads its name, then its value ("Grid, Thirds").
  • State never depends on color: a tile that is on is filled and its value is written out. In night mode on is filled red and off is outlined.
  • Largest text sizes: the existing scrolling layout with the shutter row pinned. The Q panel becomes a full-height list, one row per setting with the value at the trailing edge.
  • Siri and Shortcuts: tile names are the names the actions already use (Light, Night Mode, Interval Shooting, Focus Range, Fast Shutter), so a spoken result and the tile read the same. No new actions.

B · Reach

Reach puts every control used while shooting in the lower third of the screen, where a thumb gets to it with one hand, and leaves the top as a quiet status line. The drive modes that already exclude one another (Single Photo, the self-timer, Burst, Interval Shooting and Astro) become a strip of words above the shutter, so a use case is one tap away and its name is always on screen; tapping the selected word opens its options. Q sits beside the shutter and opens large tiles without covering the shutter row. Settings opens from the Q panel, because it is used rarely and set-once options do not need a permanent button. The cost is one more row of chrome below the picture and a second tap to reach Settings.

Accessibility, Siri and Shortcuts

  • One-handed and situational use (gloves, cold, a tripod in the dark): no control needed while shooting is above the lower third, and the main targets are 52 to 76 pt.
  • The drive strip is one adjustable VoiceOver element, "Drive", with the mode as its value; swiping up or down changes it. Voice Control uses the visible words ("Tap Astro").
  • The Q button is named "Quick Menu", with "Q" as a second spoken name. Tiles read name, then value. On is filled and written out, never color alone.
  • Largest text sizes: the strip becomes a single "Drive" menu row in the scrolling column, and the Q panel becomes a full-height list. The shutter row with Q stays pinned.
  • Siri and Shortcuts: the strip uses the words of the existing actions (the timer, Interval Shooting), and Siri answers match the strip state. Astro has no Siri action today and gets none in 1.0.0.

C · Strip

Strip treats the camera as an instrument. A row of named values under the lenses states what the next photo will use, and each value is the control that changes it, so there is nothing to open to find out how the camera is set. Q extends the same strip upward into a table of the remaining settings, in the same form, covering less of the picture than the other two panels. Manual Controls is an A/M switch at the start of the strip, and a mode such as Astro swaps in its own values. This direction is the fastest for people who change settings often and it leans least on symbols. The cost is the most text on the default screen, small labels that depend on the Large Content Viewer, and the most work to fit eleven languages and the largest text sizes.

Recommendation

B · Reach, with A · Bands as the lower-risk fallback.

  • It answers the use-case priority most directly: Astro, the self-timer, Burst and Interval Shooting are named on the camera screen and one tap away, instead of being found inside a menu.
  • It is the strongest for one-handed and situational use, which the accessibility and inclusive-design items in issue 74 ask for: every control used while shooting is in the lower third, with the largest targets of the three.
  • Its Q panel leaves the shutter row in reach and covers less of the picture than A.
  • Its extra row is in the black band, not on the picture, so the photo keeps the same size and stays uncovered in the default state.

What to check before choosing it: that five strip words fit in the eleven launch languages (they can scroll sideways, or shorten to symbols with words in the Large Content Viewer); that moving Settings behind Q does not slow first-time setup; and that the Astro card is not too large on the picture (it can use A's readout row instead). If the smallest change is preferred for 1.0.0, A gives most of the menu clean-up with the least build and test work, and B's drive strip can be added to it later. C is best kept as a reference for the Manual Controls readout row rather than as the default screen, because of its text-size and translation cost.

Not drawn yet

These options cover iPhone in portrait only. Once a direction is chosen, the full set follows: iPhone held sideways, 4.7-inch, iPad and iPhone Duo, the largest text sizes, right-to-left, light appearance for Settings, the Apple Watch remote, the other use cases (close-up, interval, bracketing and burst, self-timer and remote, the Light, manual exposure, the front camera) and the final app icon. Sample values (frame counts, estimates, lens ranges) are examples. The scenes in the viewfinder are drawn placeholders, not photographs.
