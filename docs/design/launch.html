The launch screen is what iOS draws from the moment the icon is tapped until the app has drawn its first frame. The app’s code is not running yet, so the screen is fixed when the app is built: it cannot read a setting, show a version, move, or carry text that would need translating. Apple’s guidance is that it should look like the app’s first screen, so that the app seems to open at once, and that it is not a place for a splash screen. The build specification, the evidence, the checks and the decisions are in LAUNCH-SCREEN.md.

Recommendation: option B, built on option A. The launch screen is the camera’s black, the same in the light and the dark appearance, with the icon’s mark in the middle of the window. The app keeps the mark at the same point for its first start and fades it out when the picture arrives, so the mark is part of the first screen and nothing jumps. Option A is the whole of option B without the image, so it is built first and is what ships if any device check for the mark fails.

Today

Release 0.3.0 sets no launch color and no image. iOS then uses the system background, which is white in the light appearance. The camera is always dark, so a phone in the light appearance shows a full white screen before a black one, on every launch. That is the worst case for a camera used at night, and for Night Mode.

The mark

The mark is the app icon’s artwork without its ground. When the icon is tapped, iOS grows the icon into the launch screen; the mark is in the middle of both. It is a picture, not text: nothing needs translating. It uses no font.

Why not option C

Appearance

Devices

One color and one image serve every device, window and orientation. The image is never scaled. Its center is the center of the window, not of the safe area: that point is the same whichever way the window is turned, which matters on an iPhone launched sideways.

The launch screen

Launch screen and first frames, side by side

Accessibility

  • No text on the launch screen, so nothing is left untranslated and nothing is cut off at any text size.
  • No motion on the launch screen. In the app the mark and the words leave with a 0.2-second fade of opacity, the same with Reduce Motion on.
  • VoiceOver: the launch screen has nothing to read. In the app’s first frames the one element reads “Starting the camera…”, the string the spinner has today. The mark is decorative and is hidden from VoiceOver.
  • Contrast: the words are #AEAEB2 on a solid black plate, 9.6:1. The mark is not information.
  • Night Mode: the app draws the mark and the words in the night red from its first frame. Only the launch screen itself, which iOS draws, shows the icon’s colors.
  • Largest text size: the words grow and wrap; the mark does not (figure launch-pair-largest).

Decisions for the owner

DecisionRecommendation
1Which launch screenB, built on A. A alone ships if any of the three device checks for the mark fails.
2The mark’s colorsThe icon’s own: #EDEDEE with the teal period. The period is the icon’s, not a status, so the rule that teal means a verified fact is not stretched further than the icon already does.
3The mark’s size96 pt on every device. Not enlarged on iPad.
4The mark takes the spinner’s place for the first startYes. Later starts keep the spinner. The words do not change.
5First run in the light appearanceAccept one change from black to white. It ends when camera access is granted. The other choices are worse: a launch screen that follows the system brings the white screen back on every launch.
6The Settings button in the moment before the camera state is knownNot drawn, as the figure camera-starting already shows. The code draws it today; it would appear and disappear within one launch.
7The mark on the Lock Screen camera’s first startYes: it is the same camera screen, and it costs nothing more.
8If the camera is slow to startThe mark and the words stay until it starts or fails. No timer, and no spinner added later.

Not known until checked on a device, and stated as such in the specification: how long the launch screen and the first start last; whether iOS shows a turn when an iPhone is launched sideways; where exactly iOS places the launch image; what the system shows before the Lock Screen camera’s first frame; how Smart Invert treats the launch image.
